# Beneath the Surface — Story Bible

> **Status:** Initial draft (v0.3) · **Audience:** Development team only · **Spoilers:** Contains the murderer, motive, and solution.
>
> **Purpose of this document:** The Story Bible is the single source of truth for the narrative of *Beneath the Surface*. It records what **actually happened**, why it happened, and which facts must remain consistent across dialogue, clues, locations, and game systems. Unlike a Character Bible, it is **not** written from any suspect's perspective. It is not a prompt to give to every NPC: each NPC must receive only the information that character would know or believe.
>
> **Scope:** This document owns the setting, backstory, canonical events, murder solution, and narrative logic. The **Character Bibles** expand personalities, relationships, knowledge boundaries, and dialogue behavior; the **World Bible** owns room layouts and environmental rules; the **Evidence Bible** owns discoverable clues and their exact locations. Details marked **TBD** are not yet canonical.

## 1. Story at a Glance

### 1.1 Logline

An independent scientific auditor aboard an isolated research vessel receives a cryptic warning from the expedition's lead scientist. The next morning, she is found dead beside faulty laboratory equipment. Six people had reasons to fear what she knew — but only one murdered her.

### 1.2 Core premise

- **Title:** *Beneath the Surface*
- **Genre:** Modern, character-driven murder mystery / scientific thriller
- **Setting:** *RV Meridian*, a privately operated oceanographic research vessel in the North Atlantic
- **Playable cast:** One player character; six living suspects; one murder victim
- **Inciting incident:** Dr. Elena Fischer warns the player about irregularities in experiment M-17 and promises an explanation the following morning.
- **Crime:** Elena is murdered late that night in the wet laboratory; her death is staged to resemble a machinery accident.
- **Actual murderer:** Dr. Maya Chen.
- **Central twist:** Daniel Reyes genuinely fears his unresolved equipment fault caused Elena's death, but the malfunction occurred **after** she was killed.
- **Central theme:** Nearly everyone is protecting a real secret. Guilt, deceit, and murder are not the same thing.

### 1.3 Intended player experience

The player starts with a scientific inconsistency, then faces an apparent accident, several plausible suspects, and multiple overlapping scandals. Progress depends on **comparing testimony with independent evidence**, not on persuading one NPC to announce the answer.

## 2. Setting and Research Project

### 2.1 The RV Meridian

The *RV Meridian* is a compact research vessel carrying a small, specialized expedition. It has laboratories, engineering facilities, accommodations, a bridge, and a communications system. The game depicts only the **eight people aboard**: the player, Elena, and the six suspects. Do not invent additional crew members in dialogue. Former crew members Noah Bennett and Adrian Cole (§4.4) belong only to the backstory and are not aboard.

The expedition follows a **Galway → offshore North Atlantic sampling stations west of Ireland → Galway** circuit. The player boarded in Galway on Sunday, about three days before Wednesday’s discovery. On the murder night the vessel is roughly **53°N, 16°W**, about **400 km west of the Irish coast**. Except during the 20:00–20:40 dinner hold (§7.1), it remains under power at reduced speed, adjusting heading to the sea conditions rather than anchoring or holding an exact position. Exterior research deployment is suspended; indoor systems remain powered, including the sampling apparatus.

Heavy weather prevents a safe investigator boarding/transfer window and a prompt safe return. Satellite communications remain usable, though intermittent or reduced in capacity; Sarah reports the death and coordinates with shore authorities. At the **06:50 discovery**, the latest received offshore marine forecast predicts heavy conditions for **at least through Thursday evening**. The severe-weather state persists through that period; improvement or arrival afterward is not guaranteed. The outlook comes from a received marine forecast, not the ship's radar.

**Important:** Isolation prevents outside help from arriving promptly; it does **not** make everyone legally or physically trapped forever. The ship's captain remains responsible for safety and preservation of evidence.

**Calendar:** Mid-December, with no fixed year or exact calendar dates. The mission departed Galway on Sunday with the player aboard. Elena is murdered Tuesday evening; discovery is Wednesday at 06:50, with player control beginning after the initial response. Historical incidents retain their relative dates (about a year ago, eight months ago, and several weeks ago).

### 2.2 The carbon-removal technology

The expedition tests **ocean alkalinity enhancement**: adding a prepared alkaline solution to seawater to increase its capacity to take up atmospheric CO₂. The carbon remains in dissolved forms in the water; it is not collected in a carbon-storage tank. The startup **Pelagos Carbon** intends to use the results to support publication, commercialization, and financing. The scientific approach is real; this project's implementation, performance, and scandals are fictional.

**Onboard process:** Collect seawater at sampling stations; split it into untreated controls and treated water; add a prepared **sodium-hydroxide solution** through a small flow-through dosing-and-mixing unit. The separate sampling/filtration station collects before-and-after samples and suspended particles. Researchers measure pH, total alkalinity, dissolved inorganic carbon, and particle formation over time. The weighing platform checks sample quantity, not captured CO₂. Controlled onboard tanks support experiments; open-water deployment is a separate operation suspended during the storm. No new explorable room is required.

**Main limitation:** Fixed-rate dosing and inadequate mixing create locally concentrated alkaline pockets. Under some conditions minerals precipitate into particles, removing alkalinity that should support further CO₂ uptake. Early chemical readings can therefore look encouraging while later samples show poorer retained performance. Creating capacity for additional atmospheric uptake is not proof that the predicted CO₂ has already been absorbed.

**Other limitations:** Performance depends on water composition, temperature, mixing, and air–water exchange. Average readings may hide problematic conditions close to the outlet. Environmental safety, scale-up, and net carbon benefit remain unvalidated; reagent manufacture, transport, and operations have costs and emissions. Do not describe the technology as commercially proven or universally safe.

**Plain-language summary:** “We can help seawater absorb more carbon. We haven't proved we can do it reliably, safely, and at the cost we promised.”

**Scientific knowledge boundary:** With Elena dead, **Maya is the only NPC able to explain the full scientific process, its limitations, and her improvement in detail**. Others know role-specific simplified versions: Daniel understands pumps, mixing hardware, and his own actions; Thomas understands claims and financing; Sarah understands operations; Olivia understands general safety concerns; Lucas knows the documentary-level premise. Their expertise in those areas does not give them Maya's chemical or research-model knowledge. This is not a mandatory clue order or permission for Maya to invent facts: authored records remain usable independently of her cooperation.

### 2.3 Mounting pressure

Pelagos Carbon seeks **roughly €8 million in its next funding round** to support further trials, engineering development, and a larger demonstration system. Thomas represents the prospective lead investor. Favorable expedition results matter to securing that commitment; correcting the claims could delay or derail the round and damage his credibility with other investors. This is prospective financing, not money already lost or Thomas’s personal investment. Exact commitments and terms are not fixed.

By the time the player arrives, the expedition is tense:

1. Experimental results have been overstated in investor-facing materials.
2. An earlier deployment caused ecological damage that was hidden.
3. Maya has developed a promising but insufficiently validated improvement that could make the technology commercially viable.
4. Elena is collecting evidence and is unwilling to approve publication or further deployment without proper review.

These are **three real, connected scandals**, not alternative versions of the plot.

## 3. The Player Character

### 3.1 Identity and assignment

The player is an **independent scientific auditor** contracted by an external research-funding consortium to examine the project's experimental methods and verify that raw measurements support the claims made to funders. The player boarded in Galway on **Sunday**, about three days before Wednesday’s discovery.

The player has no prior personal allegiance to the six suspects. Their legitimate responsibilities include comparing data, examining research records, and producing an independent assessment. They are **not** a police officer or an official criminal investigator.

### 3.2 Why the player becomes involved

On the evening before her death, Elena approaches the player privately. She specifically asks for an independent review of experiment **M-17** and hints that original readings have been preserved outside the official reporting workflow.

Elena's warning ensures that the player is personally invested before the murder. After her death, the auditor's legitimate work overlaps with the question of why someone wanted Elena silenced. The captain permits the player to help **preserve and interpret research evidence under supervision**, while the authorities remain responsible for the formal death investigation.

### 3.3 Pre-game event — Elena's warning

At approximately **18:10**, Elena asks to speak in the dry laboratory, away from the main group:

> “Before you sign off on anything, compare the raw readings from experiment M-17 with the results we presented to the investors. Something doesn't add up.”

When questioned further:

> “I don't want to accuse anyone until I'm certain. There's more to this than a few incorrect numbers. I'll explain everything tomorrow.”

She mentions having preserved original measurements but does not say where. She has confirmed discrepancies in M-17 and troubling older trial material, and has indications of Maya’s negotiations without yet hearing Maya’s explanation. She postpones the full briefing so the auditor can independently check M-17 while she seeks the people involved’s accounts that evening, separating mistakes from deliberate concealment before comparing findings tomorrow. She expects professional conflict, not violence, and underestimates the threat Maya perceives.

> “Check the measurements yourself. I’ll speak to the people involved tonight, then we can compare what the records show with their explanations tomorrow.”

**Player knowledge at game start:** Player control begins after the discovery response. The player remembers Elena's previous-evening M-17 warning and promise to explain more, but receives no prior knowledge of the discovery sequence, arrivals, remarks, or reactions. The player learns about the death and investigation through the opening briefing/conversations and later learns discovery details from witnesses. The warning is backstory, not a playable pre-murder scene. The player does not know the cause of death, earlier ecological incident, or Maya's negotiations.

### 4.1 Elena's position

Elena is exacting, ethically serious, and increasingly alarmed. She understands the scientific stakes and does not want to destroy a potentially useful technology without evidence, but she refuses to approve unsupported claims or an unsafe deployment. She mentored Maya and once trusted Daniel's engineering judgment; both relationships have become strained.

Her decision to confide in the independent auditor is calculated: she wants a witness outside the company's reporting hierarchy. She does **not** foresee exactly how or when she will die.

### 4.2 The three primary suspects

- **Thomas has motive:** Elena may expose manipulated performance claims and jeopardize financing.
- **Daniel has motive and apparent means:** Elena may expose the ecological cover-up, while an actual equipment fault makes his involvement look plausible.
- **Maya has motive and opportunity:** Elena has learned of Maya's unauthorized attempt to transfer research to a competitor and intends to intervene.

All three conflicts are real. **Only Maya's conflict escalates to murder.**

### 4.3 Sarah and Lucas: secondary secrets

Several weeks before the murder, Thomas pressured Sarah to finish one final seawater-sampling operation before leaving a station. Despite worsening weather and her preference to stop, she allowed the deck team to continue. Nobody was injured, but she later felt she had accepted unnecessary risk to protect the schedule. Ashore a few days before the current voyage, she admitted this to Elena while discussing Thomas’s pressure and asked her to keep it out of the audit. Elena said the decision belonged in the assessment of operational pressure. Sarah fears exposure of poor command judgment and initially minimizes the decision as routine. Elena’s note is the only discoverable record; Sarah can explain the context when questioned. Exact dates, station, and technical operating details are unnecessary.

Elena’s note: “Sarah admitted she let the final sampling operation continue despite her weather concerns, under pressure from Thomas. She asked me to leave it out of the audit. Revisit this with her.”

Over the last few weeks, Elena repeatedly noticed Lucas continuing to record after agreed sessions ended or apparently recording informal conversations, and challenged him several times. He described it as harmless background material or claimed he had not realized the session was over. Elena told him consent also applied to informal footage and that they needed to review what he had retained. He fears losing trust, access, and control of his material; no explicit career-ending threat is established. The identities of recorded speakers and exact content are unnecessary background details. This is not automatically the murder-night movement recording. Maya and Sarah independently noticed apparently continuing recording but did not challenge or report it. When specifically asked about his filming, they may say, “There were times I thought the camera was still running after we’d finished. I never checked.” Neither can infer file contents or prove a recording from that suspicion.

Elena’s note: “Lucas has continued recording beyond agreed sessions more than once. I have raised this with him repeatedly. Ask what he has retained and arrange a review of the footage.”

### 4.4 Olivia’s past reporting lapse: Noah Bennett’s injury

Eight months before the murder, former sampling assistant **Noah Bennett** suffered a deep cut and a fracture to one finger when a heavy equipment-case lid slipped while he handled sampling supplies. He left for treatment and recovery, without permanent disability. The lid’s securing catch had previously been flagged as unreliable. Former equipment coordinator **Adrian Cole** had postponed replacement, expecting careful handling to suffice until a replacement arrived. Olivia described an ordinary handling accident and omitted the prior warning to protect Adrian from what she considered unfair disciplinary blame. She now acknowledges that deciding which circumstances mattered was not hers to do. Appropriate medical care is not in question. A warning does not establish that the injury was certainly preventable, but the story does not claim respecting it would have made no difference. Adrian and Noah are historical characters, not aboard or additional investigation witnesses. This incident is separate from C-2 and the current sampling-cover failure.

Elena, Olivia, Maya, Daniel, Thomas, and Sarah know the basic injury, departure, and recovery history. Lucas joined later and initially knows nothing, as does the player. Among the onboard cast, only Elena and Olivia initially know the omitted warning and Olivia’s reason. During the last week Elena raised the incident with Olivia, who acknowledged the omission without a major confrontation; Elena said the account should be corrected. Detailed historical reports are unavailable aboard and inaccessible to Olivia at present. She remembers the central omission and motive, not exact dates, wording, or the reporting chain; these peripheral details and Elena’s earlier research route need no additional evidence design.

**Disclosure:** Elena’s note reads only: **“Noah Bennett — follow up on the incident.”** It names neither Olivia nor the injury and makes no allegation. When explicitly asked about Noah or that incident, Maya, Daniel, Thomas, and Sarah recall the injury/departure/recovery and suggest asking Olivia. They do not know the hidden circumstances. Olivia explains the accident, omission, reason, and recent discussion with Elena honestly when asked, without a confrontation, proof threshold, or elaborate evasion. She does not volunteer it in greetings or unrelated discussion. Lucas cannot supply prior knowledge. Reading the note does not automatically trigger dialogue or brief the crew. The note shares Elena’s preserved notes; their storage/access remains TBD.

## 5. The Three Scandals — What Really Happened

### 5.1 Scandal A: Manipulated experimental results

M-17 compares several runs of the existing treatment process. Raw records show variable performance: initially encouraging chemical changes, followed in some runs by particle formation and loss of retained alkalinity. **Thomas knowingly approved a misleading investor summary** that selects the strongest early readings, omits poorer runs as “unrepresentative,” removes later readings showing deterioration, and presents modelled potential atmospheric CO₂ uptake as verified removal. His misconduct is selective reporting and misleading interpretation, not an established alteration of instrument readings.

The investor account claims consistent performance and commercial readiness that the full records do not support. Elena preserves originals; revision history and approval correspondence establish deliberate selection. The precise values, document text, and author/approval chain remain **TBD**, but the manipulation mechanism is fixed.

Elena notices discrepancies and begins comparing the raw readings, summaries, and revision history. Thomas knows disclosure could ruin the financing round and implicate him in deliberate deception. He confronts Elena, but **does not kill her**.

**Narrative function:** Provides an early, convincing financial motive and a legitimate reason for the player to audit the data.

### 5.2 Scandal B: The previous ecological incident

**About one year before the murder**, during the project's early trials, the *Meridian* tested the dosing system at **Trial Station C-2**, a fictional coastal site off the outer approaches to Galway Bay. Daniel wanted to finish the planned trial before the available testing window closed. Repeated flow-monitor interruptions led him to override the low-flow dosing safeguard and rely on manual monitoring. Water flow then fell while dosing continued, releasing insufficiently diluted alkaline water. Follow-up samples showed **localized harm to the plankton community** near the discharge; no large-scale ecological disaster is established.

Sarah knew there had been a problem with the trial. Daniel told her the release had been stopped and the situation was under control. She accepted that account and did not know about the safeguard override, the adverse biological findings, or the misleading report. Elena later asked Sarah about that old trial. Sarah could offer little beyond Daniel's assurance because she had considered the problem resolved; Elena did not explain her detailed findings to Sarah. Her awareness of the interruption and Elena's inquiry does not make her a participant in the cover-up.

Daniel omitted the override from his maintenance account, described a monitoring irregularity, and helped exclude the adverse findings from the final incident summary. He rationalized this as protecting an early-stage project that could still be fixed. **Later, while reviewing earlier trials in preparation for the current scientific assessment, Elena compared the archived flow/maintenance record and biological follow-up with the final summary.** As lead scientist, she recognized the discrepancy and uncovered the concealed harm. Her 21:50 confrontation makes Daniel fear exposure.

This incident is separate from Noah Bennett's injury and the current sampling-cover failure. The approximate date, fictional site, cause, concealment, Sarah's limited knowledge, and Elena's discovery route are settled. Exact species, concentrations, calendar dates, and site coordinates are unnecessary for the story and are not outstanding decisions. The player does **not** need to inspect the historical technical records. A short entry in Elena's preserved notes raises the old incident and Daniel's involvement, leading to explicit questions for Daniel and Sarah. The note and Sarah's recollection establish a lead and Elena's renewed interest, not independent proof of every concealed detail. Daniel may reveal more under his disclosure rules. Only note access and any further admission conditions remain **TBD**.

Past maintenance problems with the wet-lab sampling apparatus are common knowledge among Elena and all six suspects, but initially unknown to the recently arrived player. This general reputation does not establish that anyone besides Daniel knows the specific unresolved fault, its severity, or that the apparatus remains unsafe to operate. Any further individual knowledge must be explicitly defined.

On the murder night, Daniel also knows a sampling apparatus in the wet laboratory has a serious, unresolved fault. He failed to document or address it properly. He leaves the laboratory before Elena's fatal confrontation with Maya.

Later, a **23:10 automated equipment cycle malfunctions**. After Elena is found by the apparatus, Daniel assumes his negligence might have caused the death. He hides records and becomes evasive. He is genuinely guilty about his conduct, but he is **wrong about the cause of Elena's death**.

**Narrative function:** Provides a powerful false lead, an emotionally believable quasi-confession, and an explanation for suspicious alterations to maintenance records.

**Established apparatus and failure.** The wet-lab sampling apparatus is an automated seawater sampling and filtration station. Pumps and a rotary selector route water from before and after the experimental treatment to sampling/filter lines. A weighing platform checks collected sample quantities by mass. Its weekly verification cycle is required by the project's operating procedure, not a universal scientific-machine rule.

An earlier interrupted verification cycle remained pending. The murder day was not its normal weekly test day. During his final maintenance check before leaving at 22:15, Daniel restored automatic operation without clearing the pending task and left the service cover raised. The controller resumed that task at its configured 23:10 maintenance window. Daniel did not deliberately arrange an accident; he neglected the unsafe cover and the pending-job state.

The broad metal service cover is hinged and held open by a worn support catch that can slip. Daniel knew it needed repair. Pump vibration during the resumed cycle dislodged the catch and the cover dropped shut, creating a brief metallic clunk. It has no cover-position sensor, so the controller recorded the resumed cycle but did not detect or log the physical cover drop or issue an alert. Mechanical inspection, not the log alone, establishes the cover failure. Both lab doors were closed at 23:10; their tight closure and ordinary ship noise masked the brief sound from everyone elsewhere. Alibis and camera/audio design must preserve that outcome.

The specific catch defect and Daniel's restart error are not shared crew knowledge. Maya knew only the general maintenance reputation when she improvised the scene and did not know the pending cycle would resume. The cover failure is not established as related to Noah Bennett's injury.


### 5.3 Scandal C: Maya's hidden improvement

**Maya's improvement is an adaptive dosing-and-mixing controller**, paired with a revised mixer that spreads reagent addition across several stages. Rather than dosing at a fixed rate, it adjusts to actual seawater flow and changing sensor readings, reducing concentrated alkaline pockets. The valuable package comprises control software, mixer drawings, experimentally derived operating parameters, and internal test datasets.

Early controlled tests show better retention of added alkalinity and less particle formation, but only over limited conditions and observation periods. It is a promising engineering improvement, not a new chemical law or a proven safe commercial system. Elena wants independent replication, longer observation, and ecological testing before deployment. Maya feels her contribution has been overshadowed and fears the company will take its credit and control.

Maya has begun secret **email negotiations** with **Nereid Technologies**, a competing marine carbon-removal company. She offers the unpublished control code, mixer drawings, operating parameters, and internal test datasets in return for compensation, a leading research position, and responsibility for developing the system. The draft agreement envisages delivery after final agreement; a completed package transfer is not established. Some correspondence predates this voyage; intermittent onboard connectivity permits later exchanges. Exact email dates, wording, and authorized discovery route remain **TBD**. The precise legal ownership of the improvement is disputed or unclear, but transferring confidential project material without authorization would breach her obligations. Negotiations and a draft agreement are established; a completed transfer is not established.

Elena knows about the negotiations, but has not seen the draft agreement that the player later discovers during the investigation. She intends to prevent the transfer and report the negotiations through the project's oversight process. Maya learns of Elena's intention to report them only during their fatal confrontation and sees it as an attempt to take away the first opportunity she has had to control her own career.

**Narrative function:** This is the **real murder motive**, discoverable only after the player moves beyond the obvious data-fraud and equipment theories.

## 6. The Murder — Canonical Solution

### 6.1 Who, why, and where

- **Murderer:** Dr. Maya Chen
- **Victim:** Dr. Elena Fischer
- **Location:** Wet laboratory, *RV Meridian*
- **Approximate time of death:** **22:45**
- **Motive:** Stop Elena from exposing and blocking Maya's unauthorized technology transfer; resentment and fear escalate the confrontation.
- **Weapon and method:** A **2 kg stainless-steel reference mass**, a squat cylinder with a defined circular rim, normally used to check the sampling station's weighing platform. During the argument beside the calibration bench, Maya impulsively picks it up and strikes Elena once on the side of the head as Elena turns away. Elena collapses; Maya does not plan the attack in advance.
- **Fatal injury (author canon):** A localized scalp laceration over a depressed skull fracture, with severe underlying brain injury causing death at approximately 22:45. Olivia's initial visual observations do not establish the full internal injury or exact death time.
- **Premeditation:** None. The deliberate attack is impulsive; both the murder and the subsequent staging are improvised.
- **Staging:** Maya alters the immediate scene to make the death resemble an accident near faulty sampling equipment.

### Elena’s evening conversations and Maya’s reasoning

**21:10 — Thomas, mess hall after dinner.** Following Elena’s refusal to endorse publication, Thomas confronts her over the financing consequences. Elena challenges the omission of later poor results and presentation of predicted uptake as verified removal; she demands correction and says she will tell the auditor. Thomas argues that the results show potential, investors understand uncertainty, and delay threatens financing and the project. Elena does not reveal Maya’s negotiations or Daniel’s incident to him. Lucas and Sarah see them conversing but do not hear the details; their observations establish the meeting, not its content or intensity.

**21:50 — Daniel, his workshop.** Elena confronts him with the mismatch between the C-2 records and final summary: the low-flow override and omitted biological harm. Daniel acknowledges his decision and concealment to her more fully than he initially will to the player, defending his belief that manual monitoring would keep things under control. Elena distinguishes the unsafe decision from leaving its consequences out of the report. She requires an accurate account and offers him a chance to give his explanation, without promising secrecy. They do not discuss the current sampling-cover fault. Before leaving for the workshop, Elena tells Olivia and Thomas in the mess hall that she is going to speak to Daniel about an old report. They can report her stated intention and direct the player to Daniel, but did not witness or hear the workshop conversation. Daniel can confirm it occurred; neither Olivia nor Thomas knows its private content. Daniel subsequently goes upstairs for the final apparatus check described in §7.1 and leaves the wet lab at 22:15.

**22:35 — Maya, wet laboratory.** Before leaving the dry lab for Daniel’s workshop, Elena requested this private discussion of Maya’s research and future shortly after half past ten; Maya accepted. Maya comes intending to defend her autonomy and persuade Elena not to interfere, without planning violence or knowing Elena intends to report the negotiations. Elena asks what material she offered; Maya first calls it career exploration, then argues that the work is hers. Elena acknowledges her contribution but distinguishes credit from permission to transfer confidential project material. Elena then first announces her intention to report the negotiations. She has not seen the draft agreement.

Maya experiences reporting as an immediate threat to her career, independence, and opportunity. She wants Elena to withdraw that decision. When Elena turns away as if the discussion is over, Maya feels dismissed and impulsively strikes to hurt and stop her in anger and panic. The deliberate attack is not a worked-out plan to erase evidence. After Elena collapses, Maya clings to the future-tense promise to report and hopes it has not yet been sent. She does not know the contents or location of Elena’s preserved notes, measurements, or research-revision reference, or whether other reporting has already occurred. General knowledge of equipment problems gives her an improvised accident explanation that she hopes will avert immediate suspicion and buy time; she neither knows nor anticipates the 23:10 cycle. Her belief that Elena left her no acceptable future is self-justification, not the story’s judgment.

### 6.2 Sequence of events

1. Following Elena’s earlier request to discuss her research and future, Maya meets Elena in the wet laboratory at approximately **22:35**.
2. Elena confronts her about the negotiations and tells Maya for the first time that she will report them to the relevant oversight authority.
3. The exchange becomes personal. Maya believes Elena intends to control or erase her scientific achievement.
4. Around **22:45**, beside the calibration bench, Maya takes the 2 kg reference mass and impulsively strikes Elena once on the side of the head as Elena turns away. The blow causes the fatal head injury defined in §6.1.
5. Maya arranges Elena beside the sampling chamber to suggest she was inspecting it when its service cover fell. She returns the reference mass to the calibration station and leaves the cover raised. This is an improvised cover-accident explanation, not a plan relying on the later cycle. Exact body orientation, residual traces, and moved items remain **TBD**; the body is outside the cover's actual sweep.
6. At **23:10**, the controller resumes the pending verification cycle. Vibration releases the worn support catch and the raised cover drops shut. No one hears the brief clunk outside the closed labs and no alert is generated. The cover does not strike Elena's body; its closed position later reinforces the apparent accident.
7. When Elena is found the next morning, the visible scene and equipment logs initially point toward an industrial accident (detailed scene traces remain **TBD**; discovery and response are fixed in §7.2).

**Non-negotiable distinction:** The 23:10 fault is real, and Daniel bears responsibility for neglecting it, but **Elena was already dead**. Maya did not need to cause the fault; she exploited the presence and reputation of unreliable equipment.

### 6.3 Why the solution is fair

The player must be able to establish all four of the following:

1. **Motive:** Elena knew about Maya's unauthorized transfer and was going to stop it.
2. **Opportunity:** Maya was at or near the wet laboratory during the relevant window, despite a contradictory account.
3. **Method:** Physical and medical evidence supports a fatal blow, not the sampling apparatus malfunction.
4. **Exclusion of the apparatus:** The documented 23:10 malfunction cannot account for Elena's fatal injury. Proving that she died before 23:10 is not required. Her actual death at approximately 22:45 remains fixed author canon.

No single clue should be sufficient by itself. Confession is optional; the evidence must be capable of supporting the correct accusation even if Maya never confesses (**TBD**: how does the game end?).

## 8. Knowledge and Misconceptions

A character can state false information because they are **lying**, **mistaken**, or **unaware**. Those are different conditions and should remain distinct.

| **Maya** | Her negotiations and draft agreement, Elena's objection, and the murder | Conceals the confrontation and stages an accident | Full details of every other scandal need not be known |

## 10. Investigation Structure

The player may explore and question suspects in different orders. The following **acts** describe a preferred dramatic progression, not a rigid dialogue tree.

### Act I — The suspicious accident

- Begin player control after the discovery, retaining Elena's previous-evening M-17 warning as backstory.
- Learn of her death and ask witnesses what happened; discovery details are not preloaded.
- Meet or question the six suspects; inspect initial evidence.
- The faulty apparatus creates immediate suspicion around Daniel.

**Player question:** Was Elena's death truly an accident?

### Act II — Everyone has a reason

- Find evidence of altered results and Thomas's financial involvement.
- Uncover the earlier environmental incident and Daniel's safety failures.
- Test testimonies against records and one another.

**Player question:** Which of the genuine scandals was connected to the death?

### Act III — The hidden improvement

- Discover Maya's breakthrough and secret negotiations.
- Learn Elena intended to stop the unauthorized transfer.
- Potentially hear Daniel's guilt-ridden, misleading admission.

**Player question:** Is Daniel's apparent responsibility real, or has the investigation been misdirected?

### Act IV — Reconstruction and accusation

- Establish from the injury and verified apparatus behavior that the malfunction did not cause the fatal injury.
- Connect Maya to the laboratory during the critical window.
- Assemble motive, method, opportunity, and contradictory statements.
- Present a reasoned conclusion to the captain for the arriving investigators.

**Player goal:** Accuse the correct person **and support the accusation with evidence**.

## 11. Narrative and Gameplay Rules

These are **canon-preservation rules**, not yet a technical design specification.

1. **One fixed solution.** Maya murdered Elena. Dialogue generation must never switch the murderer.
2. **Six living suspects only.** The playable cast does not expand through improvised crew, visitors, or surprise witnesses.
3. **No omniscient NPCs.** Each character has restricted knowledge and may have incorrect beliefs.
4. **Secrets are independent of murder guilt.** Thomas's fraud and Daniel's cover-up remain true even after Maya is identified.
5. **Physical facts are stable.** The murder time, apparatus malfunction time, and method do not change based on conversation.
6. **Evidence outranks persuasion.** A correct accusation requires corroborating clues, not simply a high trust score or a spontaneous confession.
7. **Player autonomy.** The player is an independent auditor, not secretly an accomplice or a pre-existing friend of Elena.
8. **Elena's warning is meaningful.** Her M-17 request begins a credible chain that eventually leads to the hidden improvement and motive.
9. **No unsupported inventions.** NPCs may improvise phrasing, emotions, and harmless atmosphere, but may not add decisive evidence, new historical events, or new people.
10. **Reasonable investigative authority.** The player can review audit material and help preserve evidence, but restricted access and official criminal decisions require the captain/authorities.
